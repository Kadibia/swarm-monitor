Honey bees tell nearby bees where flowers are through a waggle dance. The angle of the dance shows the direction relative to the sun. The length of the dance signals how far away the flowers are.
Rice grows best in warm, wet conditions and is often planted in flooded fields. The standing water helps control weeds. Farmers drain the fields shortly before harvest.
The printing press, introduced in Europe in the fifteenth century, lowered the cost of books. Literacy rose as books became cheaper. Ideas then spread faster between cities.
A suspension bridge hangs its deck from cables strung between tall towers. The cables carry the load down to anchors at each end. This design can span much longer gaps than a simple beam.
Coffee beans are the seeds of a small red fruit called a cherry. After picking, the seeds are dried, hulled and then roasted. Roasting changes both the color and the flavor.
The Sahara covers much of northern Africa and is the largest hot desert in the world. Only a small share of it is sand dunes. Most of it is rock and gravel plains.
Vaccines train the immune system to recognize a germ without causing the full disease. The body keeps a memory of the germ. A later infection is then met with a faster response.
A compass needle points north because it aligns with the magnetic field of the Earth. The field is produced by moving liquid metal deep inside the planet. Its direction drifts slowly over the years.
Public libraries lend books and also offer internet access and study rooms. Many run classes for children and adults. They are usually funded by local governments.
Bread rises because yeast eats sugar in the dough and releases gas. The gas gets trapped in a network of gluten. Baking sets that network into a soft crumb.
The Amazon river carries more water than any other river on Earth. Its basin holds the largest tropical rainforest. Thousands of fish species live in its waters.
Solar panels turn sunlight directly into electricity using layers of silicon. Output depends on how bright the light is and how clean the panels are. Excess power can be stored in batteries.
A railway timetable lists when trains leave and arrive at each station. Planners must avoid two trains using the same track at once. Small delays can spread across a whole network.
The Silk Road was a network of trade routes linking East Asia with the Mediterranean. Merchants carried silk, spices and paper along it. Ideas and religions also traveled the same routes.
Copper is a good conductor of electricity and is widely used in wiring. It is also easy to bend into shape. Recycled copper keeps nearly all of its quality.
Glaciers are slow rivers of ice that form where snow builds up faster than it melts. They carve valleys as they move downhill. Many are now shrinking in warmer years.
A weather balloon carries instruments high into the atmosphere to measure temperature and humidity. It rises until it bursts, then the instruments fall by parachute. The data feeds daily forecasts.
Olive trees can live for hundreds of years and grow well in dry summers. The fruit is pressed to make oil. Fresh olives are bitter and are usually cured before eating.
The human heart beats around one hundred thousand times each day. It pumps blood through a closed loop of vessels. Regular exercise makes the heart work more efficiently.
A lighthouse uses a powerful lamp and a lens to send a beam across the water. Each lighthouse flashes in its own pattern so sailors can tell them apart. Many are now automated.
Cotton is a soft fiber that grows around the seeds of the cotton plant. It is spun into thread and woven into cloth. The crop needs a long warm season.
Volcanoes form where molten rock rises through cracks in the crust. Some erupt gently with flowing lava, others explode with ash. Volcanic soil is often very fertile.
The metric system is built on units of ten, which makes conversions simple. A kilometer is a thousand meters. Most countries use it for everyday measurements.
Honey never spoils if it is kept sealed and dry. Its low water content and acidity stop most microbes from growing. Jars of honey have been found in ancient tombs.
A telescope gathers light so that faint and distant objects look brighter. Larger mirrors collect more light and show finer detail. Modern telescopes are often placed on mountains or in space.
Bamboo is a grass and can grow almost a meter in a single day under good conditions. It is strong for its weight and is used in building and furniture. Many forests of it regrow after cutting.
A census counts the people living in a country and records basic facts about them. Governments use the results to plan schools and roads. Most countries repeat it every ten years.
Salt has been used to preserve food for thousands of years. It draws water out of food, which makes it harder for microbes to grow. Salt was once so valuable that it was used as payment.
Migrating birds navigate using the sun, the stars and the magnetic field of the Earth. Some fly thousands of kilometers each season. Many return to the same nesting sites every year.
A dam stores river water in a reservoir behind a thick wall. The water can be released to make electricity or to irrigate farms. Dams also help control flooding downstream.
The first pencils used graphite wrapped in string or wood. Today the writing core is a mix of graphite and clay. More clay makes the pencil harder and the line lighter.
Maps use scale to show how distances on paper match distances on the ground. A small scale map covers a large area with little detail. A large scale map shows a small area in fine detail.
Tea is made by steeping the dried leaves of a plant in hot water. Green tea is dried quickly, while black tea is allowed to oxidize first. Water that is too hot can make green tea taste bitter.
Wind turbines turn the motion of air into electricity with large rotating blades. They work best on open land or offshore where winds are steady. Their output rises sharply as the wind picks up.
The abacus is a counting tool with beads that slide along rods. Skilled users can add and subtract very quickly. Versions of it have been used in many parts of the world.
Soil is a mix of broken rock, decayed plants and animals, air and water. Earthworms mix it and let air reach the roots. Healthy soil holds water without becoming waterlogged.
A postage stamp shows that the cost of delivering a letter has been paid. The first adhesive stamp was issued in Britain in 1840. Before that, the person receiving the letter often paid.
Elephants live in family groups led by an older female. They communicate with low sounds that travel long distances through the ground. Calves stay close to their mothers for years.
Glass is made by melting sand with other minerals at very high temperatures. When it cools it hardens without forming crystals. Adding different minerals changes its color and strength.
A budget lists the money a household or organization expects to earn and spend. Comparing the plan with real spending shows where costs are rising. Small regular savings add up over time.
The Great Wall of China was built in many sections over many centuries. It was meant to protect against raids and to control movement along the border. Long stretches of it have worn away.
Fermentation uses microbes to turn sugars into other substances such as acids or alcohol. It is used to make yogurt, bread, cheese and many drinks. It also helps preserve some foods.
A passport identifies its holder and their country of citizenship when traveling abroad. Many countries require it to be valid for months beyond the trip. Some now store details on a small chip.
Coral reefs are built by tiny animals that form hard skeletons of limestone. They shelter a large share of ocean fish species. Rising water temperatures can cause corals to lose their color and weaken.
A bicycle uses a chain and gears to turn pedaling into forward motion. Lower gears make climbing easier, while higher gears help on flat roads. Tires with more air roll with less effort.
Wool is cut from sheep once or twice a year and spun into yarn. Trapped air between the fibers keeps wearers warm. It can also absorb some moisture without feeling wet.
The first computers filled entire rooms and used glass tubes to switch signals. Transistors later made machines smaller, cheaper and faster. Today a phone holds far more power than those early machines.
Mangrove forests grow along warm coastlines in water that is partly salty. Their tangled roots shelter young fish and slow down erosion. They also store large amounts of carbon.
A court case begins when one party files a claim and the other party responds. Each side presents evidence to a judge or jury. The decision is then recorded and can sometimes be appealed.
Potatoes were first grown in the Andes mountains of South America. They reached Europe in the sixteenth century and became a staple crop. They grow underground as swollen parts of the stem.
A thermometer measures temperature by watching how a liquid or metal expands when heated. Digital versions use a small sensor instead. Medical thermometers are made to be accurate over a narrow range.
Monsoon winds change direction with the seasons and bring heavy rain to parts of South Asia. Farmers plan planting around when the rains arrive. A late monsoon can harm harvests.
Marathon races cover just over forty two kilometers. Runners train for months and learn to pace themselves. Water stations along the course help prevent dehydration.
Spices such as pepper and cinnamon were once among the most valuable goods in trade. They came mostly from tropical regions. Their high price drove many early voyages of exploration.
A satellite stays in orbit because its forward speed balances the pull of gravity. Communication satellites relay signals between distant places. Some orbit at a height where they stay above one spot on the ground.
Clay is shaped while wet and hardened by firing in a hot kiln. Fired pottery is waterproof if it is glazed. Broken pieces of pottery help archaeologists date old settlements.
A hospital triage nurse sorts patients by how urgently they need care. The most serious cases are seen first. Waiting times for minor problems can therefore be long.
Maple syrup is made by boiling down sap collected from maple trees in early spring. It takes about forty liters of sap to make one liter of syrup. The sap flows best when nights are cold and days are mild.
Fiber optic cables carry data as pulses of light through thin strands of glass. They can move large amounts of information over long distances. Undersea cables link the continents this way.
A seed bank stores seeds of many plant varieties at low temperatures. It protects crops against disease, war and climate change. Samples can be grown again when needed.
Concrete is a mix of cement, sand, gravel and water that hardens into a stone like solid. It is strong when squeezed but weak when pulled. Steel bars are added to help it resist bending.
